Doug Crocco

Artist and curator · Lives and works in Los Angeles, CA

Doug Crocco is a Los Angeles artist and curator whose colored-pencil drawings, prints, video and installations have been shown in New York, Los Angeles, Palm Beach, Miami, Boston and Melbourne. He has exhibited at The Happy Lion and ACME in Los Angeles, the Art and Culture Center of Hollywood, Yancey Richardson Gallery in New York, Tile Blush in Miami and, most recently, The Hole in Los Angeles. In 2014 he founded Big Pictures Los Angeles, a project space in Mid-City focused on emerging Los Angeles artists and special projects with artists from New York, Miami and Mexico City; before that he ran the online project Cult2vader.com from 2006 to 2016. His curatorial projects include NeoNatural at Steve Turner Contemporary, Transmutation and Holding Space at Big Pictures, and I Have Always Imagined That Paradise Will Be a Kind of Library at Blake and Vargas, Berlin. He received a grant from the Adolph & Esther Gottlieb Foundation and was artist in residence at the Armory Art Center, Palm Beach. He holds an MFA from Claremont Graduate University and a BFA from Florida State University.

unitedfutures

Studio of Michael “Caco” Peguero · Lives and works in Los Angeles, CA

UnitedFutures is the creative studio of digital media artist and educator Michael “Caco” Peguero, practicing at the intersection of visual art, new media and immersive environments. Trained as an architect — he holds a Master of Architecture from the Harvard Graduate School of Design — he works across augmented and virtual reality, generative systems and projection mapping. His project #SociallyReSponsorable places the viewer in a fictitious house party thrown by a favorite green beverage company, exploring the genuine pleasure brands work to elicit. Peguero is a lecturer at UCLA Architecture and Urban Design and has taught at Woodbury University; he has worked on projects by Pierre Huyghe, Hito Steyerl and Ian Cheng, and was a 2022–23 CultureHub resident in Los Angeles.

Ellen R Hanson

Midwest-born painter · Lives and works in Mexico City

Ellen R Hanson is a painter based in Mexico City whose work probes the performance of femininity — the resilience beneath the veneer of fragility, the domestic routines that cloak complex inner lives. She sketches in charcoal on elastic cotton jersey, then stretches the fabric to distort the figure before painting over it in oil, smoothing away every brushstroke. Her recent series Battle of the Carmens takes on the media-driven rivalry between figure skaters Katarina Witt and Debi Thomas at the 1988 Calgary Olympics. She has had solo exhibitions at Smart Objects, Los Angeles, and NARANJO 141, Mexico City, and her work has been shown at Heaven Gallery, Chicago, Circle Contemporary and Paris Internationale, and featured in New American Paintings. She holds a BA from Bennington College and an MFA in Painting and Printmaking from Virginia Commonwealth University.

Benjamin Cowan

b. 1985, Ann Arbor, MI · Lives and works in Brooklyn, NY

Benjamin Cowan is a Brooklyn painter who makes windows and altarpieces that echo Gothic art, often built around fragments of Old Master paintings — Giotto’s angels, Memling’s praying hands — set into cast-resin frames alongside found objects. A residency in Umbria first turned him toward historic architecture, and he has since made two painted installations for the Gothic Revival church of St. Ann & the Holy Trinity in Brooklyn Heights. His work has been shown at Deanna Evans Projects, The FLAG Art Foundation, 550 Gallery, The Painting Center and Main Projects, Richmond, and is held in the collections of the University of Scranton, the Ann Arbor District Library and the Rockefeller Archive Center. Born and raised in Ann Arbor, Michigan, he holds a BFA from the School of the Art Institute of Chicago and an MFA from Indiana University.

Meredith Sellers

b. 1988, Baltimore, MD · Lives and works in Philadelphia, PA

Meredith Sellers is a painter and writer living in Philadelphia. Conflating the painting-as-window with the infinite windows of the digital screen, her works borrow images from art history, stock photography, advertising and the news to examine systems of power and violence. She has had solo exhibitions at Fragment, New York, and Workplace, London, and has shown at ICA Philadelphia, the Rosenwald-Wolf Gallery at UArts, Take It Easy in Atlanta, Vox Populi and White Columns. She was selected for the White Columns Curated Artist Registry in 2022, and her curatorial projects include exhibitions at Crane Arts, Pilot Projects and Esther Klein Gallery. As a writer she has contributed to Hyperallergic, The Philadelphia Inquirer and ICA Philadelphia’s Notes. She holds a BFA from the Maryland Institute College of Art and an MFA from the University of Pennsylvania.

Henry Swanson

b. 1993, Dallas, TX · Lives and works in Brooklyn, NY

Henry Swanson is a Brooklyn painter whose large-scale oil paintings and mixed-media works draw on cartoons, comics and his childhood in Dallas, using slapstick and deliberate immaturity to address weighty subjects with both irony and sincerity. His figures — mascots, runway models, costumed characters — are often delicately bisected down the center, holding the childhood fear of the cartooned “other” against the adult farce of being something less true than oneself. He has had solo exhibitions at Plan X Gallery, Milan; Ross+Kramer Gallery, New York; Anthony Gallery, Chicago; Wadström Tönnheim Gallery, Marbella; Massey Klein Gallery and Anna Zorina Gallery, New York; and the Goss-Michael Foundation, Dallas. He has been a resident at the Vermont Studio Center and the Goss-Michael Foundation, and holds a BFA in Painting from the Rhode Island School of Design.

Aramis Gutierrez

b. 1975, Pittsburgh, PA · Lives and works in Miami, FL

Aramis Gutierrez is a Miami painter who works in the idiom of classical painting to address love, friendship, untimely death and corruption. His ongoing Orgy Paintings, begun in 2016, invite friends to pose in tangled compositions across Miami’s gardens, condos and swimming pools, invoking nineteenth-century French Romanticism and Impressionism. He has had solo exhibitions at Central Fine, Big Pictures Los Angeles, Spinello Projects, Tile Blush and David Castillo Gallery, and has shown at the Institute of Contemporary Art Miami, the Pérez Art Museum Miami, the Orlando Museum of Art and the Frost Art Museum. He co-founded the artist-run spaces GUCCIVUITTON (later Noguchi Breton) and Tile Blush, and received the South Florida Cultural Consortium award in 2017. His work is in the collection of ICA Miami. Born in Pittsburgh in 1975, he holds a BFA from The Cooper Union.

L

1984–2026 · Lived and worked in Los Angeles, CA

L (1984–2026) was a Los Angeles–based artist and spiritual practitioner, earlier known as Jason Metcalf and as Lazaros. Drawing on years of research into folk magic, superstitious ritual and esotericism, he made sculptures of objects suspended in mineral oil inside glass vessels — spells, each with an intended function: luck, clarity, love, protection, cosmic consciousness. He held solo exhibitions at JOAN, Los Angeles; Marlborough, New York; Stems Gallery, Brussels; AND NOW, Dallas; Martos Gallery, New York; and the Utah Museum of Contemporary Art, among others, and his work was shown at the Getty, Gagosian, Team Gallery, Venus Over Los Angeles and Documenta 15. His work was featured in Frieze, Artforum, The New Yorker, The New York Times and the Los Angeles Times, and in Taschen’s Witchcraft. The Library of Esoterica (2021).

Charlie Goering

b. 1993, Fort Mitchell, KY · Lives and works in Cincinnati, OH

Charlie Goering is a Cincinnati painter working across painting, drawing and collage, pairing unrelated objects and images into surreal juxtapositions in which arches recur as a framing device — recalling the early Renaissance and, later, contemporary artists such as Ken Price. Classically trained, he studied at the Art Academy of Cincinnati and the Florence Academy of Art before graduating magna cum laude from Laguna College of Art and Design. In 2017 he received both an Elizabeth Greenshields Foundation grant and the Manifest Artist Residency. He has exhibited in the US, the UK and China, including at Deanna Evans Projects and SHRINE in New York, Turley Gallery in Hudson, the Contemporary Arts Center in Cincinnati, Brownie Project in Shanghai and Warbling Collective in London.

Tom DeLaney

b. 1977, Tallahassee, FL · Lives and works in New York, NY

Thomas DeLaney is a New York artist and musician whose paintings and drawings combine data output from television, film, video and hi- and lo-res digital images into elegiac meditations on void, self and loss. His practice of appropriation destabilizes familiar imagery by modifying its context to reorganize meaning. He has had solo exhibitions at Big Pictures Los Angeles, The Painted Bird in San Francisco and Kill Devil Hill in Brooklyn, and a two-person show at Good Children Gallery, New Orleans; his work has been included in exhibitions at TW Fine Art, Snow Gallery, Secret Project Robot, Blake and Vargas in Berlin and Gallery SADE in Los Angeles. In 2025 he curated Possession at Snow Gallery, Brooklyn, featuring Alan Vega. He also performs in New York as Dr. Nowww. Born in Tallahassee in 1977, he holds a BFA from Florida State University.

Pandele Radu

b. 1993, Bucharest, Romania · Lives and works in Bucharest, Romania

Radu Pandele is a Bucharest artist who works across drawing, painting and installation, blending traditional techniques with computer graphics, airbrush and 3D printing. A graffiti artist since 2007, he became a prominent figure in Romania’s post-2015 muralist scene, with projects across the country and in cities across Europe, before turning his focus to studio painting. His work moves between pop icons and historical figures, rooted in art history yet open to consumer society and mass media. He has exhibited at the National Museum of Contemporary Art (MNAC) and the Museum of Recent Art (MARe) in Bucharest, the art museums of Cluj and Arad, the Ancien Musée de Peinture in Grenoble and the Romanian Cultural Institute in Lisbon, and his solo show TIGERTIGER was presented by Arsmonitor in 2025. He holds a BA and an MA from the National University of Arts in Bucharest and an MA from the University of Art and Design in Cluj.

Megan Lindeman

Transdisciplinary artist · Lives and works in Los Angeles, CA

Megan Lindeman is a transdisciplinary artist whose background is in painting. Her work is rooted in a visual investigation that aims to join the tangible with the intangible and is often informed by other fields of study such as neuroscience, linguistics and world history. She has exhibited locally and internationally, lectured on her work at the UCLA Art|Sci Center, and published art writings that have appeared at the NY Art Book Fair at The Museum of Modern Art. From 2008 to 2013 she co-ran an artist-run contemporary art gallery in Los Angeles, curating exhibitions of painting, sculpture, photography, video and performance. She joined Norco College in 2014 and has been Assistant Professor of Art there since 2017. She holds a BFA from the Rhode Island School of Design and an MFA from Claremont Graduate University.

Kenny Curwood

Artist and filmmaker · Lives and works in Brooklyn, NY

Kenneth Zoran Curwood is a Brooklyn artist and filmmaker working in film, animation and sculpture. He takes apart old movie cameras and projectors and repurposes them to photograph film one frame at a time, then processes it by hand in homemade chemistry; his brass and solder sculptures carry the same handmade, light-bound logic. A graduate of the School of Visual Arts, he has shown at Anthology Film Archives, Canada Gallery, Microscope Gallery, Foley Gallery and Safe Gallery in New York, and BBQLA in Los Angeles.
