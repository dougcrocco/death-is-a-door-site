The Premise

A door is not an ending. It is the last architecture of the living — a frame we build around the one passage we cannot see through.

Death Is a Door gathers painting, sculpture, photography and moving image made at that threshold — art that lingers in the room between two worlds rather than choosing a side.

Curatorial Statement

The room between two worlds

Every culture builds a passage for the dead — a ferry, a gate, a veil, a flight of stairs into water. The passage is never the destination. It is the interval: the held breath between what was and what comes after.

The works in this exhibition exist inside that interval. Their subjects are not death itself but its thresholds — a locked door, a boat mid-crossing, flowers already turning, fruit at the edge of rot. Beauty and decay share the same frame, because at the door they are the same event.

To stand before this work is to stand at the threshold — and to be asked, gently, to look through.

I

Death Is a Door

Doug Crocco · 2026
Metallic photo paper face-mounted to acrylic · 80 × 36 in
Edition of 3 + 1 AP

A door that cannot be opened and cannot be ignored. Crosses painted over corrosion; the chain that keeps the living out and the dead in. The exhibition's founding image and its plainest question — what waits on the other side?

II

The Ferry

unitedfutures · moving image with sound

A shrouded passenger is rowed toward a shore of cypress and stone. Every mythology keeps a boatman. Here the crossing is unhurried, almost tender — the transition as passage, not punishment.

After Arnold Böcklin's Isle of the Dead, whose original and later versions have travelled through influence, the representation of death, and process.

III

The Turning

Aramis Gutierrez · 2026
Oil on canvas · 48 × 54 in

Blackened fruit nested in flowers at full bloom. The oldest painterly warning — vanitas — rendered without judgement. Ripeness and rot are not opposites; they are the same time on a clock, read at 12.

IV

Empty (After Holbein)

Meredith Sellers · 2026
Oil and latex paint on panel · 14½ × 29 × 1¾ in

Holbein's entombed Christ, cropped down to the feet. Everything else is sealed under flat rose paint — the aperture holds only what the eye would reach last. A door left ajar exactly wide enough to see that no one is behind it.

V

Funeral Flowers

Ellen R Hanson · 2024
Oil and acrylic primer on lycra · 22 × 59 in

White flowers across a coffin, painted from a photograph and blurred until the moment slips its grip. Grief and memory work the same way — the harder we look, the softer the image becomes.

VI

Mask from Dallas

Henry Swanson · 2024
Oil on canvas · 10 × 8 in

A skull worn as a face, tied on with string beneath a broad red hat, the living body still visible at its edges. On the Day of the Dead, families make a pilgrimage to tombs and final resting places to spend time with those they have lost. The mask is a vibrant expression of ongoing communion between the living and the departed — family bonds carried across the threshold, not ended by it.

VII

Pigeon Feather, Stone, Giotto Crying Angels

Benjamin Cowan · 2024
Oil and acrylic on canvas · 20 × 16 in · integrated wood frame

Giotto's mourning angels — the first figures in Western painting to grieve like people — reappear on a leaning wedge propped on a pigeon feather and a stone. Frame, wall and picture are painted the same turquoise, so the whole object becomes an aperture. Grief balanced on the lightest possible thing.

VIII

Spell for Manifestation

Lazaros · 2018
Glass, mineral oil, mixed materials · 6½ × 6½ × 12 in

Dozens of faces — dead, in love, in tears — suspended in mineral oil inside a sealed jug. Every feeling preserved and none of them reachable. The second incarnation (detachment): the soul held in fluid, waiting to be poured into whatever comes next.

IX

Desire (After Dieric Bouts)

Meredith Sellers · 2026
Oil and latex paint on panel · 14½ × 21 × 1¾ in

Two windows cut into one teal field: the crucified hand, and the serpent. Bouts' pierced palm at one end, at the other the snake that death was supposed to settle — coiled, intact, still on its stone. The victory and the thing it was won against, held in tension across a surface that will not let them meet.

X

Three-Tiered Camera Obscura

Charlie Goering · 2024
Oil on canvas · 8 × 10 in

A devil balances on a shaft of light thrown from a clock tower, three crosses and a half-read clock face below. The smallest work in the show, and the most schematic — less a scene than a diagram of a belief. Time, judgement, and the trickster who works the hours.

XI

New Enlightened Era Coming Soon

Megan Lindeman · 2026
Plaster

An arched niche — part doorway, part headstone, part empty shrine — carries a promise in the grammar of a shopfront between tenants. The afterlife as forthcoming attraction. Faith and marketing speak the same tense: coming soon.

XII

Metamorphosis, or The Dark Night of the Soul

Charlie Goering · 2025
Oil on canvas · 72 × 72 in

A skeleton sleeps curled against the rim of a star-scored globe, arms folded under its skull like any tired body. The night is not above it — it is the thing it lies on. At seventy-two inches square, the door here is the size of a room.
